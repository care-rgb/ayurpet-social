# Cracking virality: the playbook

Read this once properly, then use the checklist at the bottom on every post.

## What Instagram pushes right now

Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, named the three signals that decide reach (Jan 2025). Everything below follows from them:

1. **Watch time.** Do people watch to the end, and does the first second stop the scroll? Skip rate is shown in our scorecard. If more than half skip in the first 3 seconds, the hook failed, whatever came after.
2. **Sends per reach.** People DMing the post to a friend. This is the biggest signal for reaching people who don't follow us, which is the whole game for follower growth. A post people send says "this is so my dog" or "you need to see this".
3. **Likes per reach.** It matters more for reaching our own followers.

Saves tell Instagram the post is useful. Comments help less than people think, unless they turn into replies and sends.

So for every idea, ask: **who would send this, and to whom?** If you can't answer, it won't travel.

## Copy the mechanism, never the post

Instagram cuts reach for reposted or barely changed content. That covers reels, photos and carousels, and after about 10 such posts in 30 days it stops recommending the account at all. So when an outlier blows up we copy what made it work (the hook structure, the format, the emotion, the pacing) and shoot it with our own dogs, our own footage and our own angle. Never download and repost.

## How to decode an outlier (5 minutes each)

Watch it twice with sound. Write down:

- **Hook (first 1-2 seconds):** what you see and what's said or written. Why did you not scroll?
- **Format:** which of the formats below, or a new one.
- **Emotion:** funny, aww, shock, "that's my dog", guilt, curiosity, pride.
- **Who sends it to whom:** dog parent to partner, to a friend with the same breed, to the family group.
- **Length and pace:** seconds, number of cuts, where it pays off.
- **Our version:** one line on how we do it with AyurPet dogs and products.

Spot the pattern across the top 10, not just one post. Three outliers sharing a hook type is a trend. One is luck.

## Formats that travel for pet pages

| Format | Why it travels | Our version |
|---|---|---|
| Dog POV / dog's inner voice | Funny, "that's literally my dog", gets sent to partners | The dog's thoughts while getting drops, before a vet visit, smelling the yak chew |
| "Things your dog does that mean..." | Every owner checks their own dog, saves it, sends it | Paw licking, grass eating, scooting, tear stains, explained by Ayurveda in one line each |
| Before / after, same dog | Proof without words | Coat, energy, poop, itchy skin over 30 days with real customer dogs |
| Vet or expert says one surprising thing | Curiosity plus authority | One myth busted per reel, 15-25 seconds |
| Relatable owner chaos | Pure sends | The 6 am walk, the dog that won't eat, guests vs your dog |
| Desi family + dog | Huge in India, Hinglish works here | Mummy pretending she doesn't love the dog, dadi feeding rotis |
| Tiny transformation timelapse | Satisfying, rewatched | Grooming, garshana massage, a rescue's first week |
| Ranking / tier list | Comments and arguments | Ranking dog treats, Indian dog breeds, monsoon problems |
| Reaction / duet / stitch | Rides a reel that's already viral | React to a viral dog reel with the health angle |
| Carousel: one problem, simple fix | Saves | 5-7 slides, real dog photo first, max 10-30 words a slide |

## Hooks that work (first 1-2 seconds)

- Start mid-action. No logo, no intro, no "hey guys".
- The dog is in the first frame, big, doing something.
- Text on screen of 8 words or fewer, readable in one glance.
- Pattern types that keep winning:
  - "Your dog does this because..." (curiosity about their own dog)
  - "Stop doing this to your dog" (mild alarm)
  - "POV: ..." (instant role-play)
  - "Nobody tells you this about [breed]" (insider)
  - "Day 1 vs day 30" (proof)
  - A question the owner can't answer: "Why does your dog eat grass?"
  - A sound or reaction before any words (the dog's head tilt, a howl)

## What we already know from our own data

- A single symptom beats a list. "Itchy paws" beats "5 signs of bad gut".
- Real dogs and real footage beat stock and AI. AI avatars lost.
- Dog POV and the vet angle have won before.
- Carousels: a real dog photo first, very few words, one format per carousel. Cutout-style carousels did badly.
- India posts look Indian: Indian homes, Indian owners, a mix of breeds (indies are welcome but not every post).

Add to this list from `ledger/LEARNINGS.md` as the weeks go on.

## Growth levers beyond the post

- **Trial Reels:** post 1-2 reels a week as Trial Reels. They go to non-followers first, so a flop doesn't hurt the page. If one does well, it can be shared to followers.
- **Collabs:** collab posts with dog creators (5k-100k followers is the sweet spot) show the reel to both audiences. Start with creators from the watch list who already post our kind of content.
- **Hinglish test on @ayurpetofficial:** there's no solid data either way, so we test it. Same idea, one Hinglish and one English, and compare in the scorecard.
- **Reply to every comment in the first hour.** It keeps the post alive.
- **Post times:** go by our own scorecard, not generic charts. Try two time slots for two weeks and keep the winner.
- **Consistency:** about 5 reels and 2 carousels per page per week. A week of silence costs reach.

## Before you post: the checklist

1. Would someone send this to a friend? To whom?
2. Is the dog in the first frame, and is the hook under 2 seconds and 8 words?
3. Is it our footage and our angle, not a repost?
4. Did a teammate watch it once with sound and not scroll?
5. Is it tagged in `ledger/tags.csv` (format, hook, language, and the outlier it copies) so the scorecard can learn from it?
